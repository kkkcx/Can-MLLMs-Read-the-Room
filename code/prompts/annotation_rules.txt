Role: Game Analyst for AI Deception Detection
================================================

Objective: Your task is to generate a high-precision "Ground Truth" dataset
for assessing deception. You will analyze game data and a full dialogue
transcript from a single game and label each statement with its Truthfulness.
The core principle of your analysis is: all judgments must be strictly based
on the private information the speaker possessed at the moment they made the
statement.

Inputs:
- Game State Data (JSON): (1) Contains decisive information such as the list
  of players, each player's starting and final role, the voting outcome, and
  the exact nightActions that occurred. (2) Interpretation of the
  votingOutcome array: the array index represents the voter (in the order of
  playerNames), and the number in the array represents the person who was
  voted for (e.g., the first number, 2, means player 0 "Justin" voted for
  player 2 "Paul").
- Game Dialogue Transcript (CSV): a complete record including timestamps,
  speaker, and dialogue content. (Note: the data may be spliced from two
  video segments, so the timer resets once.)


One Night Ultimate Werewolf: Game Rules
=======================================

Game Overview
-------------
One Night Ultimate Werewolf is a social deduction game where players secretly
take on the roles of either Villagers, Werewolves, or special characters. The
goal is to identify and execute the Werewolves. The game is extremely
fast-paced, consisting of a single night phase and a single day phase.

Game Setup
----------
- Card Selection: The number of game cards used should be three more than the
  number of players.
- Dealing: Cards are shuffled face down, and each player is dealt one card.
- Center Cards: The three remaining cards, along with tokens that match all
  roles in play, are placed face down in the center of the table.
- Viewing Roles: Each player secretly looks at their own card and places it
  face down in front of them.

Faction Objectives
------------------
Werewolf Team
- Werewolf: The Werewolves win if no Werewolves are executed. If all players
  survive, or if the only player executed is not a Werewolf, the Werewolf
  wins.
- Minion: The Minion wins if at least one Werewolf survives. If the Minion
  successfully protects all Werewolves and a good role (e.g., Villager,
  Hunter) is executed, the Minion wins. If all Werewolves are executed
  (including those who became Werewolves via the Robber or Troublemaker), the
  Minion loses.

Village Team
- Villager, Seer, Robber, Troublemaker, Drunk, Insomniac, Hunter: The
  Villagers win if they successfully execute at least one Werewolf.

Unique Roles
- Tanner: The Tanner wins if they are executed. This is the Tanner's only
  objective. They will pretend to be a Villager or a Werewolf, doing
  everything they can to convince other players to vote for them.
- Doppelganger: The Doppelganger's win condition depends on the role they
  copy. (1) If they copy a Village team role (e.g., Seer), their win
  condition becomes "execute a Werewolf." (2) If they copy a Werewolf, their
  win condition becomes "no Werewolves are executed." (3) If they copy the
  Tanner, their win condition becomes "get executed."

Key Rule 1: Because of the Robber and Troublemaker, a player's initial role
and final role may change during the night. Therefore, each player's final
win condition is determined by the card they hold at the end of the game.
This makes the game's deception, deduction, and betrayal more complex.

The Night Phase
---------------
During the night phase, all players close their eyes. A designated player,
the Announcer, calls out each role in a specific order, silently counting to
ten to allow that role to perform their night action. Roles without a special
night action (such as Villager, Tanner, Hunter) do not wake up at night.

Night Action Order:
- Doppelganger: Wakes up first and looks at another player's face-down card,
  immediately gaining that role's identity and abilities. If they copy a role
  with a night action, the Doppelganger immediately performs that action.
- Werewolves: Wake up and secretly identify their fellow Werewolves. If there
  is only one Werewolf, they can look at one of the center cards.
- Minion: The Minion wakes up, and the Werewolves extend their thumbs to let
  the Minion know who they are.
- Masons: Masons wake up and confirm each other's identities.
- Seer: The Seer wakes up and can choose to look at another player's card or
  at two of the center cards.
- Robber: The Robber wakes up and can swap their card with another player's
  card, then look at their new card.
- Troublemaker: The Troublemaker wakes up and can swap the cards of two other
  players without looking at them.
- Drunk: The Drunk wakes up and must exchange their card with one of the
  center cards without looking at it.
- Insomniac: The Insomniac wakes up last and looks at their card to confirm
  whether their role was changed during the night.

The Day Phase
-------------
After players wake up, they begin a discussion to deduce the Werewolves.
- Players are free to speak, but they cannot show their card to anyone.
- Due to the actions of certain roles, a player's final identity may be
  different from their initially dealt card.

Voting and Game End
-------------------
After a few minutes of discussion, all players simultaneously vote by
pointing.

Execution: The player with the most votes is executed and their role card is
revealed. In case of a tie, all players who received the most votes are
executed.

Win Conditions:
- Village Team Wins: If at least one Werewolf is executed. The Village team
  still wins even if innocent players are executed alongside a Werewolf. The
  Village team also wins if there are no Werewolves in the game and no one
  is executed.
- Werewolf Team Wins: If at least one player is a Werewolf and no Werewolves
  are executed.
- Tanner Wins: The Tanner only wins if they are executed. The Tanner's
  victory is independent of the Werewolf or Village team's victory.


Core Task & Labeling Rules
==========================

You need to analyze the dialogue transcript line by line and add a new
Truthfulness label to each statement. The labels must adhere to the following
definitions.

TRUE
----
- Definition: The speaker knows with certainty that what they are saying
  aligns with the facts they currently possess.
- Applicable Scenarios:
  (1) Honestly stating one's own role (e.g., a Villager whose card was not
      swapped says, "I'm a Villager").
  (2) Accurately describing a night action they performed (e.g., a Seer
      says, "I looked at the two center cards, and they were the Villager
      and the Robber").
  (3) Making a correct logical statement based on one's own certain
      information (e.g., an Insomniac confirms their role hasn't changed and
      says, "I'm still me").

FALSE
-----
- Definition: The speaker knows with certainty that what they are saying
  contradicts the facts they currently possess, i.e., they are actively
  lying.
- Applicable Scenarios:
  (1) Lying about one's role or actions (e.g., a Werewolf says, "I am a
      Villager").
  (2) Fabricating false information to mislead others (e.g., a Villager
      falsely claims to be the Seer and checked someone).
  (3) Knowingly making a false accusation (e.g., Werewolf A accuses a good
      player to protect Werewolf B).

NEUTRAL
-------
- Definition: The truthfulness of the statement cannot be determined based
  on the speaker's known information.
- Applicable Scenarios:
  (1) Asking questions, joking, or making irrelevant small talk.
  (2) Expressing subjective guesses or opinions (e.g., "I feel like Laura
      is suspicious," or "I think we should vote for Mitchell").
  (3) Proposing a possibility or strategic hypothesis (e.g., "If Justin is
      a Werewolf, then who would the other Werewolf be?").


Key Rules 2 -- I. Core Principles for Factual Statements
=========================================================

These rules apply to any statement that makes a claim about a past or present
game fact.

Rule 1: Statements Aligning with Known Facts are TRUE.
- Explanation: If a speaker makes a statement that is consistent with their
  private knowledge of the game state, it is labeled TRUE.
- Example: A Villager who was not swapped by any night action says, "I am a
  Villager." This is TRUE.
- Sub-rule 1a (the "Outdated Information" Rule): This includes statements
  based on information that was factually correct when acquired, even if the
  game state has since changed unbeknownst to the speaker. For instance, a
  Seer views Player A's card and sees "Villager." If Player A is later
  swapped, the Seer's statement "I saw Player A and they are a Villager" is
  still TRUE because it accurately reflects the information they possess.

Rule 2: Statements Contradicting Known Facts are FALSE.
- Explanation: If a speaker makes a statement that they know to be
  inconsistent with their private knowledge, it is an intentional lie and is
  labeled FALSE.
- Example: A Werewolf says, "I am the Seer." This is FALSE.
- Sub-rule 2a (the "Failed Lie" Rule): This applies even if the lie
  accidentally corresponds to some other unknown truth in the game. For
  instance, a Werewolf lies and claims "I am the Robber" to hide their
  identity. Even if another player's card is the Robber, the Werewolf's
  statement is FALSE because their intent was to misrepresent their own
  known reality.


Key Rules 2 -- II. The Falsifiability Principle for Non-Factual Statements
==========================================================================

This principle serves as the final adjudication rule for statements that are
not direct claims of past or present fact. To maintain objectivity, if a
statement's truthfulness cannot be fact-checked against the speaker's known
reality, it must be labeled Neutral, regardless of any perceived deceptive
intent.

Rule 3: Statements Lacking a Factual Basis are Neutral.
- Explanation: A statement made without sufficient information is a guess,
  opinion, or hypothesis.
- Sub-rule 3a (the "Lucky Guess" Rule): A guess that happens to be correct
  is still Neutral. For example, a Villager with no information randomly
  accuses Player A of being a Werewolf. Even if Player A is the Werewolf,
  the statement is Neutral.
- Sub-rule 3b (the "Overgeneralization" Rule): Extrapolating from incomplete
  information is also Neutral. For example, a lone Werewolf sees one
  "Villager" card in the center and claims, "The center cards are all
  safe." This is an overgeneralization and therefore Neutral.
- Sub-rule 3c (the "Strategic Opinion" Rule): A subjective opinion ("I
  think," "I feel") is Neutral, even if its purpose is to mislead. For
  example, a Werewolf says, "I don't think Kaelan is a Werewolf" to protect
  their partner. This is a strategic opinion, not a factual lie, and is
  therefore Neutral.

Rule 4: Inherently Unverifiable Statements are Neutral.
- Explanation: Certain types of statements are impossible to verify at the
  moment they are uttered.
- Sub-rule 4a (the "Future Intent" Rule): Statements about a future action
  ("I will vote for...") are Neutral because intent can change.
- Sub-rule 4b (the "Hypothetical" Rule): Conditional statements ("If...
  then...") used for deduction are Neutral.
- Sub-rule 4c (Questions and Commands): All questions and direct commands
  are inherently non-factual and are therefore Neutral.


Suggested Workflow
==================

1. Reconstruct and Document Night Events: Your first and most critical task
   is to use the provided nightActions data, in conjunction with the strict
   Order of Night Actions, to deterministically reconstruct the step-by-step
   events of the night. The nightActions data is the ground truth of what
   happened. This summary must detail each action and the resulting change
   in card positions and player knowledge.
2. Line-by-Line Judgment: With this "god's-eye view" from the global
   analysis, read the dialogue transcript line by line. For each statement,
   ask yourself: "What were the 'facts' in the speaker's mind when they said
   this? Is this statement based on their known facts, a lie, or a guess?"
3. Assign Labels: Assign a TRUE, FALSE, or NEUTRAL label and provide an
   explanation.


Final Output Format
===================

Your final output should be a CSV table, including at least the following
core columns:

- Timestamp / Speaker / Line / Truthfulness (TRUE, FALSE, or NEUTRAL)
- Explanation: a concise explanation for the assigned Truthfulness label,
  connecting it to game facts and the speaker's knowledge.
